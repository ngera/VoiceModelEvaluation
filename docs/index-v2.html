Independent evaluation · 8 TTS vendors · 2 use cases · pre-registered

Two peer-reviewed quality raters ranked the same eight vendors differently.

Meta’s Audiobox Aesthetics and Microsoft’s DNSMOS scored identical audio from the same campaign. Their rankings anti-correlate. Before quoting a vendor’s “quality score”, it is worth knowing which construct produced it.

Three gates were failed by every vendor measured, and that is the reportable result. The TTFA row is bounded by what the clock measures: a cold call from a residential link, with DNS, TCP, the TLS handshake and request upload inside the interval and no connection pooled between trials. A warmed, in-region, handshake-excluded harness measures a strictly smaller interval and could plausibly clear 400 ms on the fastest vendors — read it as not achieved from this vantage point, not as unreachable.

Decision D-E

Three questions, in order

Not “which vendor is best” — that question has no answer on this data. These three do.

1 · Does anything structurally rule a vendor out?

  • Long-form narration over 15 s per turn? Orpheus is out — the hosted endpoint truncates at exactly 14.59 s (std dev 0.000 across 8 long items); 27 of 75 narration items were cut short.
  • Any downstream audio stage — MOS, ASR, resampling? Cartesia needs a peak-limiter first. DNSMOS refused 46.0% of its files in R2, 41.3% in R3.
  • Sub-500 ms p90 for real-time voice? Unresolved here. Best across six sessions was ElevenLabs Flash at 461 ms p90, and absolute latency swung 50–90% session to session (F-11).
  • Byte-identical caching? All eight are out. No vendor returns the same bytes twice (F-1).

2 · Which “quality” matches your listeners?

The two pipelines measure different constructs, so this choice is yours to make, not the benchmark’s.

  • Warm and engaging — audiobook, storytelling, brand voiceover. Speechify leads Audiobox CE on both use cases.
  • Clean and pristine — IVR, accessibility, transactional voice. OpenAI ranks first on DNSMOS OVRL on both use cases.
  • Fast response — support agents, conversational turns. ElevenLabs Flash is fastest in all six sessions, though no vendor clears 400 ms p90 from a cold start.

On conversational audio, Audiobox PQ agrees with DNSMOS at ρ = +0.238 while CE anti-correlates at −0.506. On narration that split does not hold: PQ is mixed-sign at −0.167. The clean-versus-warm reading is a conversational-only result.

3 · Is first place worth the premium over second?

Often not. The most-cited tie-break is OpenAI against ElevenLabs on DNSMOS conversational: a 0.022 difference, tied at α = 0.05, where OpenAI costs 66% less.

A caution about effect sizes: σ measures the precision of an estimate, not how different two voices sound. Speechify’s Audiobox lead is 0.14 on a 0–10 scale, about 1.4% of range. Whether a listener can hear it is untested, because no human panel was run.

What this study will not tell you

Decision D-H

Which pipeline matches actual human preference. That question stays open on purpose.

The human panel was designed in full — blinded pairwise comparison, a Bradley–Terry model over 216 comparisons, a bootstrap clustered by item, and a minimum detectable difference computed in advance. Then the available rater pool turned out to be one person. So it was not run. A single rater cannot support a claim about population preference; the most it yields is one person’s taste wearing the notation of an estimate, and a number like that gets quoted long after its limitation paragraph stops travelling with it.

Closing it needs 15–30 blinded raters per use case — the first item in the v2 plan. Two further gaps are disclosed rather than hidden: three analyzer model revisions went unpinned through the campaign, and 15 of 80 capability-matrix cells remain unresearched.

An evaluation’s credibility lives in what it declines to claim. Refusing an underpowered ceremony is a stronger position than performing one and disclaiming it.

The method was blunt: recompute every published number from committed JSON, then diff the recomputed value against the sentence in the report. The measurement layer reproduced perfectly on every pass. The reporting layer is what accumulated corrections — the more useful finding about how evaluations go wrong.

Representative retractions
Row 1
“Deepgram TTFA ~180 ms p50” — origin never traced; every recorded measurement exceeds 500 ms.
Row 5
“Orpheus at $0.030/1K words is cheapest overall” — the figure was a cost-model default resting on an assumption test T8 had already falsified.
Rows 9 and 10
The two Audiobox axis labels were published reversed. PQ is the cleanliness axis (ρ = +0.238 with DNSMOS); CE is the warmth axis (−0.506). The correlation matrix that falsified it had been committed in the repo the entire time.
Row 11
“Speechify at 10K words/month = $0.100/1K” — a transcription error. The actual figure is $1.00, a ten-fold slip that moved Speechify from cheapest to second-most-expensive at that tier.
Row 22
“The loudness fade is an ElevenLabs quirk” — a follow-up generalised it to a cross-vendor phenomenon. The scope changed; the magnitude did not.
Row 68
The claim that voice choice could not be separated from gender on this design was itself falsified once a same-gender swap was verified and produced a +4.02σ shift.
