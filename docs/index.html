Voice AI evaluation — the 10-minute brief

Eight commercial TTS vendors, two production use cases, two independent quality pipelines, one pre-registered spec. What survives scrutiny and what doesn't.

Neeraj Gera · findings as of 2026-09-01 · page revised through 2026-09-04 alongside the numbered documents (see the retractions box below for the audit trail) · residential Windows 11 measurement environment · full scope · longer path (8 numbered docs + EXPERIMENTS)

The one thing to know: two peer-reviewed quality raters, applied to the same 8 vendors, rank them differently. Meta's Audiobox and Microsoft's DNSMOS have cross-pipeline Spearman ρ of −0.13 conversational, −0.27 narration. That's not a bug — they measure different constructs, and both are load-bearing depending on your use case. See the axis split below.

Every number on this page is derived from a committed JSON in analysis/. Every retraction is in CORRECTIONS.md.

Three questions, in order

Not "which vendor is best" — that question has no answer on this data. Instead:

Q1 · Does anything structurally rule out a vendor?

  • Long-form narration (>15 s per turn)? Orpheus out — the hosted Replicate endpoint caps output at exactly 14.59 s (std dev 0.000 across 8 long-narration items in T8; 27 of 75 narration items in the full corpus were truncated, 48 came back complete because they fit under the cap). (T8.) Whether the cap is model-intrinsic or a deployment-config parameter is untested; either way, the hosted endpoint truncates long items.
  • Any downstream audio pipeline (MOS check, ASR, resample)? Cartesia needs a peak-limiter step first — 49% of Cartesia narration files are refused by Microsoft's DNSMOS for peak-out-of-range on R2 (37/75; 50.7% on R3 = 38/75). Pooled across both use cases: 46% on R2 / 41.3% on R3. See 04 § footnote ² for the per-cell breakdown across both runs (F-4 + F-4a's independent-pipeline corroboration).
  • Sub-500 ms p90 required for real-time voice? Unresolved on our data — no measured vendor cleared the pre-registered ttfa_p90_ms < 400 gate in any session. Best measurement across all 6 sessions: ElevenLabs Flash 461 ms p90 (S4). The pre-registered gate-robustness sweep on that gate (robustness_points: [300, 400, 500, 600]) shows discrimination starts at 500 ms (ElevenLabs alone) and reaches two vendors at 600 ms (+Cartesia 529 ms) — the gate is not discriminating at 400 ms, but the pre-registered sweep names where it would (see 04 § Gate-robustness sweep). Absolute latency swings 50-90% session-to-session across 6 sessions on 4 days (F-11). Measure from your own environment, not our numbers, not vendor marketing.
  • Byte-identical caching? All 8 are out — no vendor produces the same bytes twice; every request is a fresh generation (F-1).

Q2 · Which "quality" matches your users?

This is where the two-pipeline design pays off. The two peer-reviewed MOS raters we used agree on some things and disagree strongly on others — and the disagreement is structural, not noise. See chart below.

  • Warm / engaging (audiobook, storytelling, brand voiceover) → Speechify wins Audiobox CE significantly on both use cases (7.3σ narration lead over ElevenLabs' #2 CE; not a tie). At $0.10/1K words above 100K/mo it's also 55% cheaper per word than ElevenLabs' $0.22 for a significantly-better-CE score.
  • Clean / pristine (IVR, accessibility, transactional voice) → OpenAI ties for #1 on DNSMOS OVRL on both use cases. At $0.075/1K words flat OpenAI is 50–66% cheaper than the tied alternatives (34% vs ElevenLabs $0.22 = 66% saving; 50% vs Deepgram $0.15 = 50% saving).
  • Fast response (support-agent, conversational turns) → ElevenLabs Flash is consistently fastest across all 6 latency sessions (rank portable), but no vendor clears the 400 ms p90 bar.

The Audiobox PQ axis correlates +0.24 mean with DNSMOS (agree on cleanliness); the Audiobox CE axis correlates −0.506 mean with DNSMOS (anti-correlate — measures warmth). Any single quality score is silently choosing which construct wins.

Q3 · Is #1 on quality worth the cost premium over #2?

Read from the cost table below. Most-cited tie-break: OpenAI vs ElevenLabs on DNSMOS conversational. Δ = 0.022, 1.3σ (tied at α=0.05). OpenAI is 66% cheaper for tied-on-cleanliness quality.

Between quality tiers within the same construct, σ measures precision of the estimate, not perceptual magnitude. Speechify's 3.7σ Audiobox lead is a Δ of 0.14 on a 0–10 scale (1.4% of scale). Whether that gap is audible to a listener is untested — no human perceptual panel was run (D-H).

The rank inversion in three lines:

  • OpenAI narration: last (#8) on both Audiobox axes, first (#1) on 2 of 4 DNSMOS axes. Technically pristine, but Audiobox reads it as unengaging.
  • Speechify narration: first (#1) on both Audiobox axes, mid-pack on DNSMOS. Warm and engaging, DNSMOS doesn't reward that.
  • Cartesia narration: high on DNSMOS.p808 (P.808 single-model listening-quality MOS), last (#8) on the other 3 DNSMOS axes (P.835 triad). The P.835 axes see the clipping problem; p808 doesn't.

An aggregate "quality score" hides this. That's why this project reports six signals, not one.

What I got wrong

A portfolio project that reads as "look at all the confirmations" demonstrates only that the author is good at post-hoc rationalisation. Every published claim that turned out to be falsified by the same JSONs it was derived from is logged as a retraction in CORRECTIONS.md. Each row is dated, links to the artefact that falsified it, and either names the retracting commit SHA or is marked "(this round)" — which currently covers a backlog of rows staged across multiple review rounds not yet committed to git. Committing resolves the "(this round)" markers to the SHA that lands them. Row count grows with review rounds; see the register itself for the current total.

Representative retractions (full list in CORRECTIONS.md):

  • #1 "Deepgram TTFA ~180 ms p50 / ~230 ms p90" — origin never traced; every recorded measurement is >500 ms.
  • #5 "Orpheus $0.030/1K words is cheapest overall" — the $0.030 is a cost-model default under an assumption T8 had already falsified. Honest rate is ~$0.07–0.09.
  • #9 + #10 Audiobox axis labels reversed — Audiobox PQ is the cleanliness axis (agrees with DNSMOS at ρ=+0.238); Audiobox CE is the warm-enjoyment axis (anti-correlates with DNSMOS at ρ=−0.506). The falsifying correlation matrix was committed in the repo the whole time.
  • #11 "Speechify @10K/mo = $0.100/1K words" — transcription error. Actual is $1.00/1K (10× slip that moved Speechify from cheapest to second-most-expensive at that tier).
  • #12 "Project spend ~$56" — a pre-project planning estimate published as if metered. Actual metered spend across 8 vendor accounts: ~$16.15.
  • #22 (Phase 2) "F-6 is an ElevenLabs L03 quirk" — Phase 2 Follow-up 1 generalised it to a cross-vendor phenomenon (ElevenLabs 25%, Deepgram 12%, Orpheus 12%, aggregate 6.2%). Scope changed; magnitude didn't.
  • #23 (Phase 2) "F-7's voice-swap check transfers" — Phase 2 Follow-up 4 extended the check from 1 vendor to 5, but see #25 below.
  • #25 The Phase 2 "HOLDS / SHIFTS" alt-voice verdict column used two rules — rank-preservation for Speechify, fixed |Δ|<0.15 for the other four — and neither is the project's own paired-z test. Under paired-z on n=8 items, every alt voice shifts significantly (3.3σ to 13.5σ on AB.PQ).
  • #68 (R15) The follow-up companion claim that voice-choice could not be separated from gender on this design (all four Phase 2 pairs were cross-gender) was itself falsified once T6's Speechify narration leg was verified as same-gender (wyatt_32 male → edmund_32 male, per the T6 verdict) and produces a +4.02σ AB.PQ shift on 20 matched items. So within-vendor voice-choice matters at ≥3σ even under same-gender swap; the four Follow-up 4 cross-gender pairs are the additional 4-vendor sweep, not the only voice-choice signal. F-7's heading was rewritten to reflect this.
  • Selected additional rows since #25 — #26 R2 was a cache-only replay (R3 is the fresh reference); #37 the tautological "cost 100% matched pricing.yaml" claim removed; #50 F-12 relabelled from a construct-cluster claim (which contradicted F-8) to a scorer-architecture split; #101 F-12 paired-z range corrected from 3.3-7.3σ to 2.26-7.32σ; #110 the gate-robustness sweep disclosed as the fourth pre-registered scoring component (added to 04 § Gate-robustness sweep); #126 EXPERIMENTS' Google DN.ovrl alt-voice row corrected from an unpaired-subset difference to a paired 5-item intersection Δ + inverted attribution ("pinned lost 3, alt lost 1"). Full register in CORRECTIONS.md.

How the retractions were found: every published number was recomputed from committed JSON, then the recomputed number was diffed against the sentence in the report. Six disagreed in the original audit that produced this mechanism story (the "6-of-14 headline claims" pass documented in 05_CASE_STUDY.md § What the audit found) — the retraction register has grown well past that as later review rounds recomputed further claims from the same JSONs (row count in the register itself). The measurement layer reproduced perfectly across every pass; the reporting layer is what accumulated corrections.

Why this belongs on the first page. A vendor evaluation that hides its retractions is asking the reader to trust the surviving claims on faith. Making the retraction register the first-page artefact reverses the default: the surviving claims are trustworthy because the register catalogues every claim that didn't survive.
